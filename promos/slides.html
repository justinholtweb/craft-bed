The whole idea
How tall is a tweet? min-height: 400px;  /* a guess */ Bed doesn’t answer that. It asks. min-height: 623px;  /* n=41 */

A guess that is too big leaves a hole; a guess that is too small still moves the page. So a sampled fraction of visitors beacon back what the embeds settled to at their own viewport width, and every later render reserves that.

Deferred loading

Lazy, except
at the top

Below the fold, lazy costs a little bandwidth. At the top, it costs the LCP.

  • A script embed’s loader tag is lifted out until it is wanted
  • Anything measured above the fold stays eager, at every width
  • A deferral only counts if the runtime is really on the page

Facades

Nobody has
pressed play

A video player is a whole document, spent before anybody has decided to watch.

  • A poster and a play button until somebody clicks it
  • Autoplay on the swap, so one click is still one click
  • Only posters needing no request of their own are eligible
Without a facade
The player arrives anyway
An iframe document, its scripts, and a handful of third-party connections — opened for every reader, including the ones who scroll past.
With a facade
One image, or nothing at all
The poster is derived from the URL, so building the facade costs no request of its own. Turn facadePosters off and it is drawn from CSS alone: a reader who never clicks makes no third-party request whatsoever.

Show your
working

  • Report what was measured, prune it, purge and start again
  • Ask what Bed makes of a URL before you paste it into a page
  • Aggregates only — a count, a sum, a maximum. No visitors
